Being out on the water so long living on dry land will come as a major adjustment. No more rocking to sleep, view of the open sky or battling the lakes unforgiving summer squalls. Its time to pack up and go to France for a 700 mile cycling trip. *Thank you to all of the Firecracker crew for hosting me.*

![](/images/uploads/IMG_0578-1.jpeg)

DIY bike case on the cheap, I went with duct tape and corrugated plastic. All bags were bought on FB marketplace, used bike stores and goodwill (thanks to Ryan for the bolt bag). Hopefully the carabiners and zip ties hold. The whole setup came out to $50.

![](/images/uploads/IMG_0867.jpeg)

![](/images/uploads/IMG_0816.jpeg)

*My associate who helped rig it.* 

Packing the bike was a pain in the ass, I ended up 2.5 lb over but American let it slide. Sonja, Ron and Ronald from Nearly New Cycles came in clutch. I hope it makes it in one piece. 

![](/images/uploads/IMG_0917-1.jpeg)

Ron and Ronald (left to right).

![](/images/uploads/IMG_0904.jpeg)

The route. Why France? They seem biker friendly place for beginner bike tourers and wanted to see the DDay beaches. Send recs along the way, we are going in blind.

![](/images/uploads/att.XduRjy3OiT8-Uyqg-Lo8ebh0El0pBvVvWaeKLBvm-nU-1.jpeg)

Much of the inspiration came from my friend Eichii. We met in New Orleans during his USA cross country bike ride at 80 years old! In crocs! I bought a pair and will try to follow in his croc-steps. His fortitude will be a source of inspiration I will draw on during many hard climbs. Another was my Uber driver Edwin. On my way to the airport he told me of his biking adventures in Spain and Italy. It sounded amazing and insisted I take my bike with my next trip to europe.
