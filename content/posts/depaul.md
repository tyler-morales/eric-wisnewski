DePaul basketball is why I started this blog.  Going to 20-plus DePaul games made me fall in love with college basketball like never before.  There were Iowa teams in the past (and currently) that had ignited an interest in the sport, but nothing like what I experienced in the 2025/26 season.  I saw a roster of players with three returning players start as a complete mess, losing to a Buffalo team that should have been an enhancement talent (professional wrestling term), to beating rivals Marquette twice and winning the most number of Big East games since 2007.  I am a sucker for an underdog story.  DePaul is less of an underdog.  They are more in line with a fallen giant, trying to rekindle that past success. The Wintrust Arena is harboring the potential of one of the greatest returns to greatness in college basketball history, as DePaul continues in their relevancy not only nationally, but in Chicago.  

![](/images/uploads/Boston/IMG_4799.jpeg)

Wintrust Arena opened in 2017 in the South Loop neighborhood of Chicago. Unlike neighboring Chinatown & Pilsen with their established neighborhood feel, the South Loop definitely feels like a greater extension of the Downtown Chicago Loop.  Mainly comprised of hotels, high-rises, chain restaurants, and Scientology.  DePaul moved from the Allstate Arena in Rosemont for reasons I can imagine as follows.  Crummy facilities, lack of attendance, recruitment draw, ease of accessibility due to O’Hare traffic, and congestion from the city that never sleeps, Rosemont.  The 10,000-seat mecca they ended up building is an almost perfect basketball arena.  Clear sight lines from every seat, location near both Red and Green Line CTA stops, CTA bus stops, an attached parking garage with expensive parking, a large and informative jumbotron, and an amazing sound/light system.  

What they succeed in execution, they fail in location, as DePaul’s campus is located nowhere near the stadium.  I am a huge advocate that the stadiums of the college teams should be on or within walking distance of the campus.  For DePaul, due to the size of its campus and location in Lincoln Park, it is almost impossible to build a new stadium there.  Just look at how much people freaked out about DePaul tearing down three buildings on campus for their new basketball facility.  No, seriously, look.  The same people talking about keeping the integrity of the neighborhood while a PE bagel shop opens down the block in their newly constructed $6 million homes.  THE INTEGRITY.  

Anyways, yea the stadium is not close to home.  If it were up to me, and you were asking my opinion, and this is just me speaking here, in my opinion, I would rather have a 5,000-seat stadium on campus, but that’s just me, that’s all I’m sayin’.  Imagine the fortress that this team could have there.  10,000 seats looks nice and is nice, if you can fill it.

Which brings me to the next point, the attendance/fans.  Before every game, DePaul plays a montage of the good ol’ times.  Back in the late 70’s and early 80’s being ranked number one in the nation putting a beating on UCLA.  The crowds look electric.  The city of Chicago completely backed the team, bringing the ruckus.  Then the video ends, and you look at the crowd today.  Yikes.  For these non-conference games, the Wintrust is lucky to have 3,000 people in attendance.  For Big East play, the attendance definitely takes an uptick for Marquette, St. John’s, and UConn games, but on average it’s more in that 4,000-5,000 range.  Which wouldn’t be bad if you had a 10,000-seat arena with an empty upper deck.  The student section is trying to be vocal and create a hostile atmosphere to rival what they see on Instagram. However, the majority of the students look uninterested or are there because their RA might have dragged them to the game for a floor bonding activity. 

![](/images/uploads/Boston/IMG_4810.jpeg)

Let’s talk about the band real quick.  They have like 8 people in it, and the conductor has to join in a lot.  They sound really good, don't get me wrong, but I’m pretty sure DePaul has a pretty excellent music program, and I am shocked they cannot get anyone in this band.  

DePaul needs to incentivize the students to attend the games.  They have taken strides in that department with Coach Holtman buying students’ lunches after games, buying them beers after big wins, and free coaches down to the stadium from campus.  They need to capture the hearts of Chicago as 70% of the stadium is designated for non-student seating.  The only way to do that is improvement on the court.  To their credit, they are aware of this and are making moves to be better.  Reaching highs in NIL donations and bringing in an established coach in Chris Holtman should, in theory, make this team better for the future.  There have been glimpses where you can feel the energy in that building, and it is addicting.  I am a junkie for it.

I love this team, I love this school, and I love this arena.  I have zero affiliation with the university except the fact it is nearby where I grew up.  I know before I die they will make an NCAA tournament (haven’t been since 2004), and it will make all my pain and suffering worth it.  For those who live in Chicago, I know it is unlikely that you went to DePaul, but they are part of your city and the identity of Chicago’s Basketball Greatness.  Before there was Michael Jordan, there was DePaul Basketball.  DePaul is Chicago’s Basketball Team.
